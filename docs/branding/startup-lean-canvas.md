# Collaborative startup — Lean Canvas

Date: 4 October 2026

Selected parent brand: **Alloy**. Working identity: an independent applied AI and product engineering studio.

Status: business hypotheses for discussion, not evidence of customers, demand, revenue, or an agreed company structure. Based on the supplied project documents and the group's six-project showcase.

## Positioning

We help organizations turn private knowledge and complex workflows into useful AI systems, with permission boundaries, measurable evaluation, and usable product interfaces.

Start as a services-led engineering studio. Deliver a focused engagement, learn what buyers repeatedly need, and productize the repeatable parts after demand is demonstrated. Keep Guardian RAG, AutoFT, KRONOS, Swarminal, DocViewer, and Nest Invest as proof of capabilities under the parent brand.

## Lean Canvas

| Block | Starting hypothesis | Evidence or next test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | Staff struggle to find trustworthy answers in private documents; access permissions complicate AI adoption; teams cannot easily tell whether their AI is improving. | Interview people responsible for knowledge access and AI delivery. Ask about their current workflow and recent incidents, not whether they like the proposed tools. |
| Customer segments | Organizations with restricted internal knowledge and engineering teams building internal AI workflows. Start with one segment, such as education administration or internal HR/policy teams. | The supplied documents identify education, healthcare, and legal teams as potential pilot partners; none is validated. Choose the segment where the team can reach actual users and a budget owner. |
| Unique value proposition | Private AI, built around your permissions and evaluated against your actual workflow. | Test whether buyers value permission boundaries and evaluation enough to fund an engagement. Avoid promising production readiness before delivery conditions are assessed. |
| Solution | A scoped permission-aware knowledge-assistant pilot, with cited answers, role-based access, an evaluation set, and a usable interface. Offer evaluation/drift assessment as a related service when relevant. | Guardian RAG and AutoFT demonstrate relevant mechanisms. A customer implementation still needs discovery, integration, and validation. |
| Channels | Founder introductions, Techne conversations, professional networks, targeted technical demos, and project case studies. | Track which conversations reach users, technical sponsors, and people able to approve a budget. |
| Revenue streams | Paid discovery where appropriate; a fixed-scope implementation pilot; follow-on integration and evaluation work; optional maintenance or support agreements. | Validate a paid engagement before developing subscriptions. Prices depend on scope, deployment needs, support expectations, and delivery costs; no price is established yet. |
| Cost structure | Engineering time, compute/model infrastructure, storage, deployment, maintenance, sales time, and business administration. | Record actual delivery hours and third-party costs for each engagement. Include support and evaluation time rather than estimating only the prototype build. |
| Key metrics | Qualified discovery conversations; scoped proposals; paid pilots; agreed evaluation criteria achieved; permission tests passed; pilot-to-follow-on conversion. | Set project-specific quality and access-control criteria before implementation. There are no validated baseline business metrics yet. |
| Unfair advantage | No durable moat is established yet. The immediate differentiator is complementary AI, web, and desktop engineering demonstrated through documented projects. | Build a stronger advantage through repeatable delivery knowledge, customer relationships, and reusable components validated across engagements. |

## First offer

**A private knowledge-assistant pilot for one team and one document collection.**

- Agree on the workflow, user roles, infrastructure, and document boundaries.
- Build a cited-answer experience with permission-aware retrieval.
- Create evaluation questions, including questions each role must be refused.
- Demonstrate the result, document limitations, and recommend the next step.
- Consider a four-week target only after discovery confirms that the scope fits.

Sell the engagement and outcome; the customer does not need to buy six separate tools.

## Validate before expanding

1. Select one reachable customer segment and interview five relevant people.
2. Find one repeated problem with an identifiable budget owner.
3. Propose a narrowly scoped paid pilot with agreed success criteria.
4. Compare actual delivery costs, evaluation results, and willingness to continue.
5. Decide whether to repeat the service, refine the offer, or productize a recurring need.

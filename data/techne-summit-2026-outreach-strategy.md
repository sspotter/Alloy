## The presentation

**Positioning:** “We build secure, measurable AI systems for a defined business workflow. Our projects demonstrate retrieval security, model evaluation and agent orchestration.”

**Thirty-second opening:** “We are an applied AI engineering team. One system we built answers questions from internal documents while enforcing document permissions before retrieval reaches the model. Another evaluates whether a model change is actually better. I'm looking for one team with a real knowledge or agent-quality problem where a small, measurable pilot would help. Is that relevant to a workflow you own?”

For a buyer, show one problem, one demo and one proposed success measure. For a partner, show how one capability fits their delivery work and ask whether a specialist gap exists. For an advisor, present one unresolved question. For an investor or incubator, present the chosen customer segment, prototype evidence and next validation milestone.

| Project | Best audience | Demonstration and next step |
|---|---|---|
| Guardian RAG | HR/operations teams, governed document products, enterprise implementers | Same question under two roles; allowed passages, citations and audit trail. Ask to qualify one internal-knowledge workflow. |
| AutoFT | Teams operating LLM products or agents | Baseline evaluation, drift signal, candidate comparison and a rejected release when improvement is insufficient. Ask about an evaluation audit before automated retraining. |

Keep the card structure supplied in the brief: product → value proposition → technical proof → capability tags → CTA. A source repository supports the case study; the initial conversation should make the business use case understandable. Label working prototypes, experiments and private case studies accurately. Agree who represents each project before presenting a shared team offer.

## Qualify an opportunity before committing time

Ask what workflow is painful, how often it occurs, who owns it and what current tools already do. Then establish whether suitable documents or evaluation data can be supplied, who approves the engagement, what procurement steps apply and whether there is an actual timeline and budget. For partners, ask whether the gap is delivery capacity, a missing specialty or a referral opportunity.

For Guardian RAG, a proposed initial scope is one department, one approved document set, two user roles and an agreed test set. Measure answer usefulness, citation correctness, access isolation and time saved against the current process. Use synthetic documents for the event demo. Agree acceptance criteria with the customer before promising a production deployment.

For AutoFT, start with a baseline and evaluation engagement on one existing LLM workflow. Agree the measurement method before offering retraining. Some problems will be better solved by retrieval, prompting, data quality or simpler monitoring; qualify those alternatives during discovery.

## Follow-up and success criteria

Before the event conversation, have a QR link to the portfolio, a 90-second demo with an offline backup, and a one-page brief for the relevant offer. After the conversation, record the problem, workflow owner, interest, blockers, next step and a date in the outreach tracker. Request permission to send the appropriate case study and follow up within 24 hours.

Suggested follow-up: “Good meeting you at Techne. You mentioned [specific problem]. Here is the short [relevant case study]. Would a 20-minute call with the workflow owner next week help us decide whether a small evaluation or pilot is worthwhile?”

Proposed event targets: six relevant conversations, three agreed discovery calls, two concrete technical discussions and one written pilot proposal after qualification. These are execution targets, not expected results. Use a single follow-up after 3–5 business days when there is no response; spend further time where the problem and next step are clear.
